The dependency flux as a measure of syntactic complexity: spoken and written French

Sylvain Kahane (Modyco (CNRS), Université Paris Nanterre; IUF) and Kim Gerdes (LISN (CNRS), Université Paris-Saclay). Article for the issue of Studii de lingvistică on complexity, version of 23 November 2026.

Sentence length, a common measure of complexity (François 2011), cannot be used to compare speech and writing, because the maximal syntactic unit of speech is difficult to define (Pietrandrea et al. 2014). Mean dependency length (Liu 2008; Ouyang et al. 2022) and the proportion of dependencies longer than a threshold (Niu et al. 2023) have been proposed instead. We introduce related measures based on the dependency flux. The flux at a point of a sentence is the set of syntactic dependencies that link a word before this point to a word after it. We first consider the size of the flux: its mean is equal to the mean dependency length, but its large values are much more constrained. We then consider finer measures of the composition of the flux, which could not be defined in terms of dependency length. On treebanks of spoken and written French, we show that the syntactic complexity of speech is as high as that of writing when only microsyntax is considered, that it lies in different places (in verbal rather than nominal constructions), and that it is even higher when the macrosyntactic relations, much more frequent in speech, are taken into account (Blanche-Benveniste 1990).
